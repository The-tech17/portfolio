I recently built my portfolio with AI—not because I wanted to pretend I could code everything myself, but because I wanted to see how far I could take an idea with the skills I actually have.

I wanted a portfolio that I could share with recruiters looking for profiles like mine. I gathered and incorporated the important pieces: my CV, contact information, published book covers and PDFs, and other relevant work. I also suggested changes to make the website clearer, cleaner, and easier to navigate.

The interesting part was that I didn’t write the website code myself. Antigravity handled the frontend as well as the backend. I don’t know intensive coding, so having AI translate my requirements and suggestions into a working website was something I realistically couldn’t have done alone, or at least not without spending a very long-time learning and implementing it.

But the process wasn’t completely smooth. I struggled with documenting the assets, capstone requirements, deliverables, and attachments needed for approval of my internship track. Antigravity helped substantially here too, while I worked on the remaining parts.

The biggest thing I learned is that using AI doesn’t mean handing over all responsibility to it. I still had to know what I wanted, review what was produced, identify what needed changing, and complete the parts that required my own judgment.

For me, this portfolio became less about “I built a website” and more about learning how to turn an idea into something usable with AI as a collaborator.
